This is the single most useful thing to understand about this material, and most listings get it wrong.

**Thymosin β4 (Tβ4)** is a 43-residue protein found naturally in many cell types, involved in actin sequestration. It has an extensive literature going back decades and has been through clinical trials for several indications.

**TB-500** is the name commonly used in the research-chemical market for a **short synthetic fragment** of that protein — usually the seven-residue actin-binding region, sometimes acetylated. It is not thymosin β4. It is a fragment of it.

Those are different molecules with different molecular weights, different synthesis routes and — critically — different literatures. A vendor page that lists "TB-500" and then cites thymosin β4 clinical work is attaching one molecule's evidence to another molecule's product. Check the sequence on the certificate, not the name on the label.

## What each literature covers

**Thymosin β4 (the full protein):**
- Actin sequestration and cytoskeletal dynamics, which is the established biochemistry.
- Corneal and dermal wound models.
- Cardiac injury models.
- Human clinical trials, including for dry eye and epidermolysis bullosa — these exist and are findable, and they concern the full protein.

**The short fragment sold as TB-500:**
- A much thinner literature, largely in-vitro, focused on cell migration endpoints.
- Frequently studied alongside, rather than instead of, the full protein.

The asymmetry is the point. If a claim rests on clinical data, it almost certainly rests on Tβ4 data, and the fragment in the vial is not the molecule that was tested.

## What neither literature establishes

- No approval by any major regulator for the fragment, for any indication.
- No published human efficacy data for the short fragment.
- Fragment-to-protein extrapolation is an assumption, not a finding. A sequence that binds actin in isolation does not necessarily reproduce the behaviour of the protein it came from.

## Checking what you actually received

The certificate should state the **sequence** and a **theoretical mass** consistent with it. A seven-residue acetylated fragment and a 43-residue protein differ by thousands of daltons — this is the easiest identity check in the entire catalog, and it takes thirty seconds with any peptide mass calculator. The [mass spectrometry guide](/mass-spectrometry-identity-confirmation/) walks through it.

If the certificate names only "TB-500" with no sequence, you cannot tell which molecule you are holding. Ask before ordering.
