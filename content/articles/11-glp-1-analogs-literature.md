These three are frequently listed side by side and are not equivalent — pharmacologically, or in regulatory status. If you are sourcing any of them as a reference material, the differences below are the ones that matter.

## Receptor targets

**Semaglutide** is a GLP-1 receptor agonist — a single incretin receptor.

**Tirzepatide** is a dual agonist at the GIP and GLP-1 receptors.

**Retatrutide** is a triple agonist, adding glucagon receptor activity to GIP and GLP-1.

That progression — single, dual, triple — is the whole reason the three exist as distinct molecules, and it is the correct factual framing for a listing. Each is a long-acting peptide with structural modifications that extend half-life relative to the native hormones.

## Development and regulatory status

This is where they diverge sharply, and where anyone selling them should be paying attention:

| | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Semaglutide | Approved by major regulators for specific indications, marketed under brand names, under patent |
| Tirzepatide | Approved by major regulators, marketed under brand names, under patent |
| Retatrutide | **Investigational.** In clinical trials. Not approved by any major regulator for any indication |

Two of the three are approved drugs under active patent. The third is an unapproved investigational compound. None of them is an ordinary research chemical in the sense that BPC-157 or GHK-Cu is, and listing them as interchangeable catalog items misunderstands the position badly.

## What this means commercially

Plainly, because it is not obvious from most vendor pages:

- **Patent holders enforce.** The companies developing these molecules have taken action against sellers. That exposure attaches to the seller, not to the buyer's research use.
- **Payment processing is materially harder** for these than for other peptides. Several processors that accept research chemicals exclude incretin analogs by name.
- **Advertising channels are closed.** Merchant Center and every major ad platform prohibit them, and enforcement is account-level.
- **An unapproved investigational compound is not the safer option** because it is unapproved. Retatrutide is in active trials, which means it has a sponsor with an interest in its exclusivity and regulators watching the space closely.

## What the literature establishes

For semaglutide and tirzepatide the clinical literature is large, peer-reviewed and findable — these went through full trial programmes. For retatrutide, published phase trial results exist but the programme is ongoing, which means the evidence base is genuinely incomplete rather than merely young.

In all three cases the clinical literature concerns pharmaceutical-grade product administered under supervision in a trial setting. It does not describe material bought as a reference standard, and it is not transferable to it. Anyone citing trial data next to a research-chemical listing is making a comparison the data does not support.

## Sourcing as reference materials

Where these are used legitimately — analytical method development, reference standards for chromatography, in-vitro receptor work — the requirements are the ordinary ones: sequence and mass confirmed, purity by a stated method, lot traceability, net peptide content where concentration accuracy matters. Because these are long, modified peptides, synthesis-related impurities are a real consideration and the gradient used in the purity method matters more than it does for a short sequence. The [HPLC purity guide](/hplc-purity-what-it-means/) covers why.
